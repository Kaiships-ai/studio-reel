## Chapter 1: The Doctor Who Drank the Bug

[about 1 min]

A doctor swallowed a beaker of bacteria. On purpose.

1982, Perth, Australia. Everyone knows ulcers come from stress and spicy food.

But Barry Marshall and pathologist Robin Warren keep seeing the same tiny spiral bug in sick stomachs, again and again.

The experts scoff. Reviewers rank their paper in the bottom ten percent they received that year.

The bug? Just a contaminant, they say. Harmless.

He spends six months trying to infect piglets. It fails. So, to win the argument, he picks one last test animal: himself.

1984. Two dishes of the bug go into a beaker of beef broth, and down it goes.

He doesn't clear it with the hospital, or his wife. In his words: "easier to get forgiveness than permission."

Days later, his wife notices his breath smells putrid. Then he's throwing up every morning.

Wait. A camera down his throat finds his stomach inflamed. The biopsy grows the spiral bug.

You see, the bug is behind most ulcers, not stress. It keeps the stomach lining inflamed.

Kill the bug, and the ulcer heals for good.

Two weeks of pills. No surgery.

Twenty-one years after that putrid drink, the doctor they laughed at collects the Nobel Prize.
