## Chapter Text

To win an argument, a doctor drank a beaker of live bacteria.

Western Australia, the early 1980s. Everyone knows ulcers come from stress, spicy food and too much acid.

But Barry Marshall and pathologist Robin Warren keep finding the same tiny spiral bug in ulcer patients.

The experts laugh. Reviewers rank their paper in the bottom ten percent of the year.

The bug? Just a contaminant, they say.

So he tries to infect piglets. Nothing. That leaves one test animal: himself.

July 1984. His lab tech scrapes two dishes of bacteria into beef broth, and he knocks it back like a shot.

He doesn't clear it with the hospital, or his wife. In his words: "easier to get forgiveness than permission."

Days later, his breath smells putrid. Then he's throwing up every morning.

Wait. A camera down his throat finds his stomach swollen and red, crawling with the spiral bug.

You see, the real culprit was the bug. It inflames the stomach lining, and that's where most ulcers start.

Kill the bug, and the ulcer is cured. Two weeks of pills instead of surgery.

Twenty-one years after that putrid drink, the two men they laughed at walk on stage in Stockholm and collect the Nobel Prize.
