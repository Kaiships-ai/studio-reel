# Premise workshop (story-skills `premise-workshop`, adapted)

Adaptation: the skill is built for one book with an author who chooses. Here Kai handed the orchestrator full authority and asked for ONE solution, so each "present options" step lists the options and records the pick and why.

## 1. Spark (kept in its original wording)

Kai's brief: "short narrated stories of weird true things (a pink prison, an ancient jar, railroads, pirate cannonballs): hook on frame 0, climax on the last word, a doubter + quoted line device." The stick-man kit already has a city (Halden), 14 sets and 9 cast specs. Sort: a **feeling** (the "wait, that's real?" jolt) plus a **setting** (a toy city that can play any place).

## 2. What-ifs (10)

| # | What-if | Conflict built in? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What if the same three stick actors restage every weird true story, like a tiny theatre troupe? | yes (roles: doer, doubter, witness) |
| 2 | What if every episode is an idea the world laughed at, and the last word is the proof? | yes (doubt vs proof) |
| 3 | What if Halden is cursed so true history keeps happening in it? | weak (magic hides the true part) |
| 4 | What if a stick detective checks one rumour a day: real or fake? | yes, but slows the hook |
| 5 | What if each episode is a bet between two characters? | yes, but the bet frame eats 10 s |
| 6 | What if the narrator is a character who lies once per season? | breaks the "facts are true" rule |
| 7 | What if each episode is one object with a weird true backstory? | weak doubter |
| 8 | What if the doubter is always the same man, and he is always wrong? | yes (a running gag) |
| 9 | What if we only tell disasters? | conflict yes, tone too dark for 3/day |
| 10 | What if each story is told from the odd idea's point of view? | gimmick, costs clarity |

Pick: 1 + 2 + 8 combined. Rejected 3, 6 (break truth), 4, 5 (cost seconds before the hook), 9 (tone).

## 3. Logline and stress tests

Logline: In a toy city called Halden, three stick actors restage one true story that sounds fake, and the last word proves it real.

| Test (`premise-tests.md`) | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Protagonist + want + obstacle + stakes | pass | per episode: the doer wants proof, the doubter laughs, a body or a name is at stake |
| Conflict is built in | pass | doubter vs doer every episode |
| Irony / hook | pass | "sounds fake" is a promise the last word pays |
| Repeatable (serial engine) | pass | the world supplies endless true stories; only the script swaps |
| Distinct from the reference channel | pass | our frame is a fixed troupe + "laughed at, proven right"; their stories and characters are not used |
| Risk | weak | real living people are portrayed; mitigated by sourcing every claim and keeping the tone admiring |

## 4. Premise and counter-premise

- Premise (value + cause): an idea everyone laughs at wins when it obeys a real rule the laughers never checked.
- Counter-premise (the doubter's belief): common sense is right; an idea that sounds silly is silly.

## 5. Stakes

External: the fact the world got wrong. Relational: the doubter's laugh, aimed at the doer. Internal: the doer's body or name on the line (Marshall's stomach, the Ghost Army's cover, Pullman's hotel).

## 6. Form

`serial`: each episode is a chapter of 190-250 words; a season is the book. Moving parts per episode: 1 doer, 1 doubter, 1 witness, 3-5 places, 1 hidden rule. That fits flash length, not a short story.

## 7. Titles

Sounds Fake (kept: says the promise, two words, reads in a thumbnail), Laughed At (good but negative), It Worked (spoils the end), True Somehow (soft), Nobody Believed It (long). `story names` run on the cast and Halden: 0 clashes.

## 8. Comparable work (sanity check)

Comparable format only: the four Ricks Clips reference shorts measured in `tmv/kit/records/stickman-style/FORENSIC.md` (narrated, stick 3D, ~65 s). Distinct: fixed troupe, fixed doubter, the "laughed at" frame. No book comps are claimed.
