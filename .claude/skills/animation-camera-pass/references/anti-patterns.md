# Camera Anti-Patterns

Use this list in Step 1 (diagnosis) and Step 6 (review). Each entry: symptom, why it fails, fix. Error codes refer to SKILL.md §3.1.

---

## A. Shot-level anti-patterns

### AP-01 Early close-up (C)
- **Symptom**: the tightest framing of the scene appears in the first third.
- **Why it fails**: visual intensity peaks before the story does; the climax has nowhere to go.
- **Fix**: hold the early beat at medium or medium-close; save the tightest size and the most extreme lens for the climax shot.

### AP-02 Medium-shot plateau (C)
- **Symptom**: four or more consecutive shots at the same size, lens and eye-level angle while the story escalates.
- **Why it fails**: full affinity; the audience feels nothing building.
- **Fix**: step the size tighter with each beat, push lens from 35 toward 85/135, or introduce a low angle on the character gaining power.

### AP-03 Drone camera (E)
- **Symptom**: the camera flies, orbits or cranes freely with no character or story motivating the move.
- **Why it fails**: the camera becomes the subject; reads as "CG demo".
- **Fix**: lock off, or tie the move to a motivation (follow a character, reveal information, express an emotional shift). Use a physical rig with ease in / out.

### AP-04 Wide lens on emotion (D)
- **Symptom**: 18-24mm on a close-up of an emotional face.
- **Why it fails**: distorts features (big nose, receding ears), often reads comedic or grotesque.
- **Fix**: 85mm or longer for emotional close-ups; keep wide lenses for space, action or deliberate unease.

### AP-05 Angle contradicts power (D)
- **Symptom**: the character losing the argument is shot from low angle, the winner from high angle.
- **Why it fails**: the camera tells the opposite story from the performance.
- **Fix**: low angle for whoever holds power in the beat; flip the angles when power shifts, and make the shift visible.

### AP-06 Two focal points (A)
- **Symptom**: action and important information compete in different parts of the frame.
- **Why it fails**: the audience looks at the wrong thing and misses the beat.
- **Fix**: one focal point per shot; use size, contrast, isolation, depth of field and leading lines to point at it; split into two shots if needed.

### AP-07 Unreadable silhouette (A)
- **Symptom**: a key pose overlaps the body or another character, or the camera views it head-on.
- **Why it fails**: the action does not read, especially at small screen sizes.
- **Fix**: move the camera to a three-quarter or profile angle where the pose reads as a clear shape.

### AP-08 No lead room (A / D)
- **Symptom**: character looks or moves toward the near frame edge.
- **Why it fails**: feels cramped and accidental.
- **Fix**: leave space in the direction of look or travel. Remove lead room only on purpose (trapped, cornered).

### AP-09 Impossible storyboard angle
- **Symptom**: a boarded shot cannot be reproduced in 3D with a lens from the kit without clipping walls or distorting the set.
- **Why it fails**: the board promised a frame that does not exist; layout improvises.
- **Fix**: validate the frame in proxy previs before approving the board (SKILL.md Step 5).

## B. Cut-level anti-patterns

### AP-10 Crossing the line on a reverse (B)
- **Symptom**: in shot / reverse-shot, both characters appear to look the same direction.
- **Why it fails**: the audience loses spatial orientation.
- **Fix**: keep all setups on one side of the 180-degree axis; to cross, move the camera across within a shot or have a character cross the axis on screen.

### AP-11 Jump cut by accident (B)
- **Symptom**: consecutive shots of the same subject from almost the same angle and size.
- **Why it fails**: reads as a glitch.
- **Fix**: change angle by at least 30 degrees or change size by at least one step.

### AP-12 Flipped screen direction (B)
- **Symptom**: a character travels left-to-right, cuts, then travels right-to-left with no turn shown.
- **Why it fails**: looks like the character turned back.
- **Fix**: keep direction; show the turn, or cut through a neutral head-on / tail-away shot.

### AP-13 Eyeline mismatch (B)
- **Symptom**: a character looks up-left, the next shot reveals the object down-right.
- **Why it fails**: the connection between look and object breaks.
- **Fix**: plan eyeline targets on the floor plan; match look direction and height across the cut.

### AP-14 No release after climax (C)
- **Symptom**: after the peak, shots stay tight, fast and extreme.
- **Why it fails**: exhausting; the climax does not land.
- **Fix**: after the peak, return to affinity: wider, longer, steadier shots.

## C. Process anti-patterns

### AP-15 Generic feedback
- **Symptom**: "the camera isn't working" with no shot or feeling attached.
- **Fix**: require feedback as `shot_id + error_code + intended audience feeling` (SKILL.md Step 6).

### AP-16 Fixing shots in isolation
- **Symptom**: each shot is polished alone; the sequence still feels wrong.
- **Fix**: always review the full sequence first; re-plot the intensity graph after every round of changes.

### AP-17 Redrawing instead of testing
- **Symptom**: rounds of prettier storyboards, no 3D test.
- **Fix**: move to proxy previs as soon as options are chosen; judge playblasts in the animatic timeline.

### AP-18 Camera changes after animation starts
- **Symptom**: camera tweaked once animators have posed to it.
- **Why it fails**: every cheat-to-camera breaks; animation must be redone.
- **Fix**: enforce CAM LOCK; any later change goes through the director with a cost estimate.

### AP-19 Lens soup
- **Symptom**: a different arbitrary focal length in every shot (27mm, 42mm, 63mm...).
- **Why it fails**: no consistent visual language; intensity becomes unreadable.
- **Fix**: restrict to the project lens kit.

### AP-20 Starting without director intent
- **Symptom**: options are produced before anyone states what the audience should feel.
- **Why it fails**: every option is a guess; review becomes taste.
- **Fix**: stop and get one sentence of intent per scene (SKILL.md §6).
