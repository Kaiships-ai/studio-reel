# Explanation

**Status: improving.** It works, but it is not yet as strong as a listicle. The usual weak spot is the
middle, where the mapping slows down. Keep every mapping to one line and one visible action, and
report what worked.

## When to use

The idea is how something works: an API, interest rates, a sourdough starter. The reel explains it through one
picture everyone already knows (a restaurant, a post office, a garden), mapping each part of the picture to one
real part. `styles/kai-hand-draw/examples/02-api-explained.json` is a finished script in this shape.

## Beats

| Beat | 30 s (3 parts) | 45 s (4 parts) | 60 s (5 parts) | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | 0–3 s | 0–3 s | The everyday moment where the viewer meets the thing, and a promise to make it simple |
| The picture | 3–7 s | 3–8 s | 3–9 s | "Picture a restaurant." The whole metaphor world, at a glance |
| Mappings | 7–24 s, ~5.5 s each | 8–37 s, ~7 s each | 9–50 s, ~8 s each | One part of the picture = one real part ("You're at the table. That's your app.") |
| The reveal | 24–27 s | 37–41 s | 50–55 s | One line that names the whole thing ("That waiter is the API.") |
| CTA | 27–30 s | 41–45 s | 55–60 s | One action, exactly what they get |

- **One metaphor for the whole reel.** Never switch pictures halfway; every mapping lives in the same world.
- **A prop travels** through every mapping (the order ticket, the parcel, the seed), so the camera has a reason to
  move and the viewer follows one thing.
- **Map, don't define.** Each line says what a part does, in the picture's words first, then names the real part.
- **The reveal pulls back** to show the whole picture at once, now labelled.
- If the picture needs a caveat ("unlike a real waiter, an API never forgets"), give it one line, at most once.

## Hook patterns

- "You use <thing> every day. Nobody explains it like this."
- "<Thing> sounds complicated. It's just a <everyday picture>."
- "Here's <thing>, explained like you're five."

## CTA rule

The keyword names the topic; the viewer gets the next explanation or a one-page cheat sheet. A follow CTA
("Follow for more tech, explained like you're five") also fits this type when there is nothing to send.

## Styles that suit it

Hand-drawn & Painting (whiteboard, one-line drawing, watercolour), Information & Keynote's Isometric Infographic
(systems), Graphic & Type's Blueprint (machines), Materials & 3D (a paper pop-up or tabletop world), Cartoon & Anime
(characters play the parts).

## A worked outline: "what an API is" (about 28 s)

1. Hook: "You use APIs every day. Nobody explains them like this."
2. The picture: "Picture a restaurant." Table, waiter and kitchen doors in one wide shot.
3. "You're at the table. That's your app."
4. "The kitchen has the food. That's the server, with all the data."
5. "You can't walk into the kitchen, so a waiter takes your order and brings back your food." The ticket travels.
6. Reveal: "That waiter is the API." Pull back; every part labelled.
7. CTA: "Follow for more tech, explained like you're five."
