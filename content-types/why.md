# Why

**Status: new.** Not yet proven by a finished reel; follow the beats closely and report what worked.

## When to use

The idea is a question about cause: why something fails, why it works, why people do it, why it costs what it costs.
The reel answers with two or three reasons, each backed by one piece of evidence, then turns the answer into
something the viewer can use. If the idea is about how something works rather than why, use
[explanation](explanation.md).

## Beats

| Beat | 30 s (2 reasons) | 45 s (3 reasons) | 60 s (3 reasons) | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | 0–3 s | 0–3 s | The surprising claim or the failure itself, shown |
| Stakes | 3–6 s | 3–7 s | 3–9 s | Why the viewer should care: what it costs them |
| Reasons | 6–24 s, ~9 s each | 7–36 s, ~9.5 s each | 9–48 s, ~13 s each | "Reason one: <name>." One piece of evidence, shown |
| The turn | 24–27 s | 36–41 s | 48–55 s | What to do about it, in one line |
| CTA | 27–30 s | 41–45 s | 55–60 s | One action, exactly what they get |

- **Each reason is a name plus one proof**: a number from a named source, a real example or a short demonstration.
  No reason without evidence; cut it instead.
- **Order the reasons** so the least expected one comes last.
- **The turn is the payoff.** It flips the problem into an action ("so give it one job, not ten").
- Keep the tone curious, not alarmist; one claim per line, no absolute promises.

## Hook patterns

- "<Thing> fails <real share> of the time. Here's why."
- "Everyone thinks <common belief>. That's not why <thing> happens."
- "Why does <everyday thing> <surprising behaviour>? Three reasons."

## CTA rule

The keyword names the fix; the viewer gets the checklist, the sources or the longer write-up
("Comment AGENTS and I'll send you the checklist").

## Styles that suit it

Evidence Films (when the buyer has real data or imagery), Information & Keynote's Data Storytelling (when the
reasons are numbers), Print & Printmaking's Halftone Dossier (a case file), Graphic & Type (one reason per card),
Cinema & Eras (a mystery tone).

## A worked outline: "why AI agents fail" (about 42 s)

1. Hook: an agent's to-do list goes red line by line. "Most AI agents fail at the same three things."
2. Stakes: "If you're building one, these are the hours you lose."
3. "Reason one: too many jobs." One agent juggling ten tasks drops them; evidence from a named benchmark or the
   buyer's own logs.
4. "Reason two: no memory of what it did." It repeats the same step; a real transcript excerpt.
5. "Reason three: nobody checks its work." It reports done on a broken result; one real example.
6. Turn: "So give it one job, a notebook and a checker."
7. CTA: "Comment AGENTS and I'll send you the checklist."
