# Education

**Status: proven.**

## When to use

A short lesson on ONE thing (a tool, a method, a recipe, a skill), taught as a named process of three to five steps,
each with one real example. Not a feature list (that is a [listicle](listicle.md)), not a hype explainer and not an
install tutorial. The viewer should be able to do the first step right after watching.

## Beats

| Beat | 30 s (3 steps) | 45 s (4 steps) | 60 s (5 steps) | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | 0–3 s | 0–3 s | The result first: show what the method produces, then name it |
| What it is | 3–6 s | 3–7 s | 3–8 s | One line: what the thing is and who it is for |
| Steps | 6–24 s, ~6 s each | 7–37 s, ~7.5 s each | 8–50 s, ~8.5 s each | "Step one: <name>." Then one real example of that step, shown |
| Takeaway | 24–27 s | 37–41 s | 50–55 s | What to do with it today, in one line |
| CTA | 27–30 s | 41–45 s | 55–60 s | One action, exactly what they get |

- **Name the process** ("the three-pass plan", "the 4-step menu test") and say the count early, so the viewer knows
  how long the lesson is.
- **The lesson object is the subject of every shot**: the notebook, the screen, the dish. A host or presenter stays
  small (about a third of the frame width at most) and never covers it.
- **Every rule, number or step name is fully on screen** and held sharp at least 0.5 s on its word. Readable beats
  matter more than motion here; a slower, steadier camera than a listicle is right.
- Each step's example is real (a real input and its real result), not a placeholder.

## Hook patterns

- Proof first: "This <result> took me <real time>. Here's the <N>-step method."
- "Most people use <thing> wrong. Here's the fix, in <N> steps."
- "<Named method>: <N> steps, and you can try the first one today."

## CTA rule

The keyword names the method; the viewer gets the written steps, a template or a checklist they can reuse
("Comment PLAN and I'll send you the template").

## Styles that suit it

Hand-drawn & Painting (a whiteboard or sketchbook lesson), Information & Keynote (software on screen), Graphic &
Type and Blueprint (rules as diagrams), Cartoon & Anime (a host teaches), Evidence Films' Exploded Teardown (one
product, part by part).

## A worked outline: "plan your week in 10 minutes" (about 40 s)

1. Hook: a full, colour-coded week fills in at high speed. "This plan took ten minutes. Here's the three-pass method."
2. What it is: "It's a way to plan a week on one page, for anyone who keeps running out of day."
3. "Pass one: dump." Every task on one list; a real messy list appears.
4. "Pass two: three big rocks." Three tasks get circled and moved to the top.
5. "Pass three: block." The rocks land in morning slots on the week grid.
6. Takeaway: "Do pass one tonight. It takes two minutes."
7. CTA: "Comment PLAN and I'll send you the one-page template."
