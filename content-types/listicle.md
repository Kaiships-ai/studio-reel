# Listicle

**Status: proven.** The strongest format in the library; `styles/kai-hand-draw/examples/03-tools-listicle.json` is
the shape behind a 92k-view reel.

## When to use

The idea is a set of separate things, each worth knowing on its own: tools, apps, tips, places, mistakes, dishes.
Three to seven items. Each item needs a name and one concrete benefit for the viewer. If the items are steps toward
one result, use [education](education.md) instead.

## Beats

| Beat | 30 s (4 items) | 45 s (5 items) | 60 s (7 items) | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | 0–3 s | 0–3 s | Who it is for and what they get, with the count ("these four free tools") |
| Items | 3–26 s, ~5.5 s each | 3–40 s, ~7 s each | 3–55 s, ~7.5 s each | Item name alone first ("One: Ponytail."), then one or two lines on what it does for *you* |
| CTA | 26–30 s | 40–45 s | 55–60 s | One action, exactly what they get |

- **One chapter per item.** Each opens with its number and name on screen, large, landing on the spoken name.
- **A continuity prop** runs through every item and fills up as the list goes: a score board gets a row, a shelf
  gets a jar, a map gets a pin. It tells the viewer how far along they are, and the last item completes it.
- **One world for the whole reel** (an arcade, a kitchen, a workshop); each item is a new corner of it, never a new
  film. Pick the world from the items, and never reuse the world of the last reel.
- **Something new on screen every 1–1.5 s**, with each beat held long enough to read (at least 0.35 s). Vary how
  items hand over (a wipe, a push through an object, a match cut), but keep one move per handover.
- At most one number per item, said the way people say it ("fifty-four percent less code").

## Hook patterns

- "Don't start <thing> until you've seen these <N> <items>."
- "<Brand or field> has <N> <items> that are completely free."
- "<N> <items> I use every single day, and the last one saves me <real number>."

## CTA rule

The keyword names the list ("Comment TOOLS and I'll send you all four links"). The viewer gets the full list with
the links or details the reel left out.

## Styles that suit it

Cartoon & Anime (a host walks the list), Games (each item a level, a cabinet or a card), Graphic & Type (one item
per grid card), Information & Keynote (software and products), Print & Printmaking (one print per item).

## A worked outline: "4 free tools for small online shops" (about 32 s)

1. Hook: "Running a shop alone? These four free tools do the boring part for you." A till drawer bursts open on frame 0.
2. "One: <tool>." The first jar lands on the shelf; one line on the hours it saves, from the tool's own page.
3. "Two: <tool>." The second jar; one line on what it does for the viewer.
4. "Three: <tool>." The third jar.
5. "Four: <tool>." The last jar completes the shelf, and the shop lights come on.
6. CTA: "Comment SHOP and I'll send you all four links."
